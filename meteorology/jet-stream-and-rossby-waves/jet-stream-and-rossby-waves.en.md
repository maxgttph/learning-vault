# Jet Stream and Rossby Waves

## TL;DR

> The jet stream is a ribbon of very fast air near the tropopause, the signature of a strong horizontal temperature contrast (the "thermal wind"). Its meanders — **Rossby waves** — arise from the conservation of vorticity on a rotating planet, and they steer both the birth and the track of depressions.

## Key concepts

- **Jet stream** — a ribbon of westerly wind at ~9–12 km altitude, $100$ to $>400\ \text{km/h}$.
- **Polar jet (~50–60°)** — sitting on the polar front, the strongest and most sinuous; it governs our weather.
- **Subtropical jet (~30°)** — at the top of the Hadley cell.
- **Thermal wind** — the wind strengthens with altitude wherever the horizontal temperature gradient is strong.
- **Rossby waves** — meanders of the jet (4 to 6 around the globe) caused by conservation of vorticity.
- **Vorticity** — absolute vorticity = planetary ($f$) + relative; conserved by an air parcel.

## Deep dive

### What the jet stream is and why it exists

A narrow ribbon (a few thousand km long, a few hundred wide, a few km thick) blowing from **west to east** near the tropopause. Two jets per hemisphere: **subtropical** (~30°, top of the Hadley cell) and **polar** (~50–60°, polar front).

Its existence comes from the **thermal wind**: the more two neighbouring air masses differ in temperature, the more the wind strengthens with altitude at their boundary. The polar jet therefore sits exactly on the polar front (cold polar air / mild subtropical air) and is **stronger in winter**, when the contrast peaks. Concrete consequences: it **steers and feeds** depressions (upper-level divergence zones that suck air up from the surface), acts as a west-to-east rail, and explains the asymmetry of transatlantic flight times.

### Why the jet meanders: Rossby waves

The Coriolis parameter $f = 2\Omega\sin\phi$ **increases from south to north**. An air parcel conserves its **absolute vorticity** (planetary $f$ + relative vorticity). Hence a restoring mechanism:

- Pushed **north** → $f$ increases → relative vorticity must **decrease** → clockwise (anticyclonic) rotation → the parcel is **sent back south**.
- Overshooting **south** → $f$ decreases → relative vorticity increases → anticlockwise rotation → **heads back north**.

Result: a **self-sustaining** north–south oscillation — the jet undulates. These are the **Rossby waves** (planetary waves), typically 4 to 6 around the globe. They can be **anchored or triggered** by terrain (the Rockies, the Tibetan plateau) and by land/sea contrasts.

### Why it matters: troughs, ridges and blocking

- **Trough** (a meander dipping south) = a tongue of cold air → favours depressions.
- **Ridge** (dipping north) = warm air, high pressure, fair weather.

When the jet undulates strongly and **slowly**, you get **blocked** patterns (persistent heatwaves or cold spells). Arctic warming is suspected of making the jet wavier, because it **reduces the north–south temperature contrast** that keeps the jet taut.

## Examples & analogies

- **The latitudinal spring**: any north–south displacement of a parcel creates a restoring force that brings it back and sets it oscillating — exactly like a mass on a spring.
- **The meandering river**: the jet is a river aloft; its meanders drop cold air to the south (troughs) and warm air to the north (ridges), like a watercourse eroding its banks.
