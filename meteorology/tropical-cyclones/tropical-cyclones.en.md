# Tropical Cyclones (Hurricanes)

## TL;DR

> A tropical cyclone is a thermodynamic engine fuelled by the heat of a warm ocean ($> 26.5\ °\text{C}$) — warm-core, frontless — radically different from a mid-latitude depression (cold-core, fuelled by temperature contrasts). Both spin anticlockwise (N. hemisphere) thanks to Coriolis.

## Key concepts

- **Tropical cyclone** — warm-core, frontless system born over a warm ocean; Caribbean peak in **August–September**.
- **Eye / eyewall** — calm centre (sinking air) ringed by a wall of thunderstorms carrying the strongest winds.
- **Latent heat** — energy released by condensation: the cyclone's fuel.
- **African easterly wave** — a disturbance out of the Sahel, the seed of many Atlantic hurricanes.
- **Extratropical transition** — a hurricane travelling north can turn into a mid-latitude depression.

## Deep dive

### Two different engines

| | Mid-latitude depression | Tropical cyclone |
|---|---|---|
| Energy source | **Horizontal** temperature contrast (baroclinic) | **Latent heat** from the warm ocean (thermodynamic) |
| Thermal core | **Cold** | **Warm** |
| Fronts | Yes (warm, cold, occluded) | **None** |
| Latitude | 40–60° | 5–20° (never on the equator) |
| Season | Mostly winter | Late summer (warmest ocean) |

The tropical cyclone is a **Carnot engine**: it pumps heat out of the warm ocean, releases it by condensation in the eyewall, and dumps it at the cold tropopause. Hence the need for water $> 26.5\ °\text{C}$ → Caribbean peak in **August–September**, once the tropical Atlantic has banked the summer's heat.

### The indispensable role of Coriolis

Without the Coriolis force there is no organised rotation. Since $f = 2\Omega\sin\phi$ vanishes at the equator, **no cyclone forms within ~5° of latitude of it**. This is the same physical cause as the rotation of our mid-latitude depressions (see [[lows-and-highs.en]]).

### Structure

- **Eye**: near-calm, clear centre (20–50 km), with air **sinking**.
- **Eyewall**: a ring of giant thunderstorms, **maximum wind and rain**, tops reaching 15–18 km.
- **Rainbands**: showers organised into spirals.
- Circulation: spiralling **inwards** at the surface (anticlockwise, N. hemisphere), venting **outwards** at the top.

### Links to the general circulation

Many Atlantic hurricanes are born from **African easterly waves** carried west by the **trade winds** — the lower branch of the Hadley cell, on the southern flank of the **Azores high** (see [[general-atmospheric-circulation.en]]). That high acts as a wall steering them towards the Caribbean. Some later "recurve" around the western edge of the high, travel north and undergo an **extratropical transition**: they lose their warm core and become ordinary depressions — hence the "ex-hurricanes" that occasionally sweep across Europe in autumn.

Finally, the link to [[nao-and-enso.en]]: **El Niño reduces the number of Atlantic hurricanes** (it increases the wind shear that decapitates nascent cyclones); **La Niña favours more of them**.

## Examples & analogies

- **The oceanic steam engine**: the warm ocean is the boiler, condensation in the eyewall is the expansion stroke that releases the energy, the cold tropopause is the cold sink.
- **The eye of the spinning top**: at the centre of a fast-spinning top, the axis is strikingly calm — like the clear eye of the cyclone.
