# Modes of Climate Variability: NAO and ENSO

## TL;DR

> The NAO (a pressure seesaw between the Azores and Iceland) governs Atlantic winters; ENSO (El Niño / La Niña, in the equatorial Pacific) is the planet's largest driver of year-to-year climate variability. Key difference: the NAO is **atmospheric** (fast, barely predictable), ENSO is **ocean–atmosphere coupled** (slow, predictable months ahead).

## Key concepts

- **NAO (North Atlantic Oscillation)** — pressure seesaw between the Azores high and the Icelandic low.
- **ENSO** — El Niño – Southern Oscillation; a coupled ocean–atmosphere phenomenon of the equatorial Pacific.
- **El Niño / La Niña** — warm / cold phases of the eastern equatorial Pacific.
- **Walker circulation** — east–west circulation along the equatorial Pacific; weakened by El Niño.
- **Teleconnection** — the remote influence of a climate mode on weather in other regions.

## Deep dive

### The NAO

A pressure seesaw between the two centres of action of the North Atlantic: the **Azores high** (south) and the **Icelandic low** (north), measured by the pressure difference between them.

- **Positive NAO**: both poles strengthened → steep gradient → **strong westerlies** → storm track aimed at northern Europe.
  - Northern Europe/UK/Scandinavia: **mild, wet, stormy** winter; Mediterranean/Iberia: **dry**; Greenland: cold.
- **Negative NAO**: gradient collapsed → weak or blocked westerlies → continental/Arctic cold air floods into Europe.
  - Northern Europe: **cold and dry** winter, cold spells; Mediterranean: **wet**; Greenland: mild.

The phase of the NAO reflects the state of the **jet** (zonal and strong vs. wavy/blocked) — see [[jet-stream-and-rossby-waves.en]].

### ENSO (El Niño / La Niña)

A phenomenon of the **equatorial Pacific**, coupling ocean and atmosphere:

- **El Niño**: trade winds weaken → warm water sloshes back east → eastern Pacific anomalously **warm** (off Peru) → convection (rainfall) shifts to the central Pacific; the **Walker circulation** weakens or reverses.
- **La Niña**: the mirror image — strengthened trades, **cold** eastern Pacific.

### NAO ↔ ENSO side by side

| | NAO | ENSO (El Niño) |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Atmospheric **pressure** seesaw | Coupled **ocean + atmosphere** seesaw |
| Region | North Atlantic | Equatorial Pacific |
| Exact analogue | NAO ↔ **Southern Oscillation** (the pressure half of ENSO, a Tahiti–Darwin seesaw) | |
| Timescale | Weeks to months, irregular | 2 to 7 years, more rhythmic |
| Predictability | **Low** (atmosphere ≈ fast chaos) | **Good, months ahead** (ocean memory) |
| Reach | Mainly Atlantic/Europe | **Global** |

Key difference: the NAO is **essentially atmospheric** (fast, barely predictable); ENSO is **coupled to the ocean**, whose thermal inertia buys several months of predictability. The cleanest structural analogy is **NAO ↔ Southern Oscillation** (two pressure seesaws); the big difference is that on the Pacific side the **ocean drives**, while on the Atlantic side it is mostly the **atmosphere**.

### Consequences of El Niño (teleconnections)

- **Peru/Ecuador**: torrential rain, flooding; collapse of the **upwelling** → devastated fisheries (the origin of the name).
- **Indonesia, Australia, India**: droughts, wildfires, **weakened monsoon**.
- **Atlantic**: **fewer hurricanes** (increased wind shear) — see [[tropical-cyclones.en]].
- **Globally**: a generally **warmer** year (global temperature records often fall in El Niño years).
- **La Niña**: mirror effects — **more Atlantic hurricanes**, drought in the southern United States, rain in Australia/Indonesia.

## Examples & analogies

- **Two seesaws**: the NAO and the Southern Oscillation are two pressure seesaws (Azores↔Iceland, Tahiti↔Darwin). But the Pacific seesaw is weighted down by the ocean (slow, predictable), while the Atlantic one is light (fast, capricious).
- **The ocean's memory**: the atmosphere forgets within days; the ocean, which stores heat, "remembers" for months — that memory is what makes El Niño predictable.

## Open questions

- To what extent does ENSO influence the NAO (a Pacific → Atlantic teleconnection)?
- How does climate change alter the frequency/intensity of extreme El Niño events?
