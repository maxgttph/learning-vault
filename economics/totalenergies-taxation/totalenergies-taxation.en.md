# TotalEnergies and Tax: Making Up Your Own Mind

## TL;DR

> TotalEnergies almost never pays corporate income tax in France — that is a fact, and it is legal: France taxes only profits earned on its territory, where the group does nothing but refining and distribution, structurally loss-making. The real debate is therefore not "fraud or not" but **where the profits are located, and whether the territoriality of French corporate tax is still a defensible political choice?** The friction has moved to a single accounting line: "rest of the world" (Switzerland + Singapore), a quarter to a third of profits, taxed at around $10$–$15\ \%$.

## Key concepts

- **Territoriality of corporate income tax** (art. 209 of the French tax code, *CGI*) — France taxes only the profits of businesses operated in France. Unlike the pre-2017 United States, no worldwide taxation.
- **Parent-subsidiary regime** — dividends flowing up from subsidiaries are $95\ \%$ exempt (a $5\ \%$ share for costs and expenses) to avoid double taxation.
- **Carry-forward losses** — losses from French refining are set against future profits, wiping out corporate tax in the good years.
- **Taxes borne vs taxes collected** — the French fuel excise duty (*TICPE*) and VAT pass through the company but are borne by the customer. Conflating them artificially inflates the "contribution".
- **Transfer pricing** — the prices of transactions between subsidiaries of the same group; the main lever for locating profit.
- **Pillar 2 (OECD)** — a $15\ \%$ global minimum tax, in force in France since 2024. A floor… which also becomes a de facto ceiling in trading hubs.
- **CbCR** — country-by-country reporting; public in the EU (directive 2021/2101) from financial year 2026 onwards.

## Deep dive

### The French facts, uncontested

TotalEnergies paid no corporate income tax in France in 2019, 2020, 2021, 2023 and 2024, and expects none for 2025 — while 2024 worldwide net income reached €$14.6$bn (down $21\ \%$). For 2025, the only profit-based contribution is a tax on share buybacks, estimated by the group at €$100$–$150$m.

The mechanism is public and legal:

1. No hydrocarbon production in France — hence none of the extraction profits, which make up most of the result.
2. The French activities (refining, petrochemicals, distribution) are structurally barely profitable or loss-making.
3. Accumulated losses are carried forward, the research tax credit reduces the balance further, and tax consolidation nets losses and profits across the French perimeter.

The group even escaped the exceptional surtax on large groups in the 2025 budget, for the same reason: no taxable base in France.

### What the group puts forward

Since 2022 TotalEnergies claims some \$$100$bn in profit taxes and production royalties paid worldwide, at an average effective rate of $43\ \%$ — $40\ \%$ and a little over \$$19$bn for 2025 alone, more than $51\ \%$ and nearly \$$20$bn in 2022. Producing countries apply specific petroleum regimes taxing at between $50$ and $80\ \%$.

In France, the company announces "more than €$2$bn" of contribution to public finances in 2025. **This is where you have to read the footnote**: that total aggregates taxes *borne*, employer social contributions and levies *collected*. In 2022, the CEO put forward €$1.6$–$1.9$bn in France, of which €$1.1$bn was social contributions and €$500$m withholding tax on dividends — sums paid on behalf of others, not a tax on its profits.

The group's substantive argument: taxing in France profits already taxed in Nigeria or Norway would violate bilateral tax treaties against double taxation. Legally, that is correct.

### The hot spot: the "rest of the world" line

The serious criticism no longer concerns extraction, but **trading**. Two subsidiaries — Totsa (oil, Geneva) and TotalEnergies Gas & Power (gas, London/Geneva) — buy and resell molecules they do not produce. They reportedly generated €$3$bn and €$2.5$bn of profit in 2023, about $30\ \%$ of the group's profits.

In the tax transparency report, those profits sat in a "rest of the world" aggregate that Patrick Pouyanné had to acknowledge, before the National Assembly finance committee on 17 June 2026, as corresponding to Switzerland and Singapore.

The figures put forward by Gabriel Zucman (note of 30 July 2026):

- 2022 — \$$11.5$bn of profits on that line (a quarter of worldwide income), \$$514$m of tax, i.e. $\frac{514}{11\,562} \approx 4.4\ \%$; about $10\ \%$ if you use tax *accrued* rather than paid.
- 2024 — \$$5.9$bn of profits, effective rate $\approx 15\ \%$.
- 2019-2024 cumulative — an effective rate below $15\ \%$ on that aggregate.
- A telling order of magnitude: roughly \$$1$m of profit per employee present in those jurisdictions.

The group's answer: Switzerland and Singapore are the world's commodity trading hubs, where the entire sector operates, the economic activity there is real, and the $15\ \%$ rate is exactly the OECD minimum. Talking about tax avoidance would be "simply false".

**This is the heart of the disagreement**: both camps agree on the rate ($\approx 15\ \%$) and on its legality. They differ on whether a trader that neither extracts nor consumes can legitimately capture a third of an oil group's margin.

### The European anomaly

A frequently cited 2022 comparison: France accounted for $51\ \%$ of the group's European sales but $4\ \%$ of its European taxes; Germany, $19\ \%$ of sales for $43\ \%$ of the taxes (around €$600$m). A gap of that size is not explained by activity mix alone and deserves digging into the statutory accounts rather than being accepted or dismissed wholesale.

Another mismatch documented by the 2023 report on payments to governments: United Arab Emirates $28\ \%$ of payments for $14\ \%$ of production; Norway $17.5\ \%$ of payments for $9.6\ \%$; Nigeria $8.8\ \%$ of production for $5\ \%$ of payments. In value: Norway \$$4.9$bn, United Kingdom \$$2.3$bn, Nigeria \$$1.2$bn — the top 5 concentrate $75\ \%$ of payments.

### The "windfall profits" episode

The European temporary solidarity contribution (EU regulation 2022/1854, transposed in the French 2023 budget act) taxed at $33\ \%$ the portion of 2022 profit exceeding the 2019-2021 average by more than $20\ \%$. French outcome: €$61$m collected in total, against €$200$m expected — while Italy raised €$2.8$bn and Spain €$2$bn. The same cause produces the same effect: with no taxable base in France, no yield. A device targeting profits *declared in France* cannot reach a group whose profits are elsewhere.

### The four debates not to be mixed up

The "Total" file aggregates four questions with no factual connection to one another. Treating them separately is the precondition for a solid opinion:

| Debate | Nature of the question |
|---|---|
| Tax | Where are the profits located, and at what rate? |
| Rent / windfall profits | Industrial performance or geopolitical windfall? |
| Climate | Production trajectory vs. stated commitments |
| Geopolitical ethics | Russia, Mozambique, EACOP in Uganda |

Answering a tax criticism with a climate argument (or the reverse) is a rhetorical shift, not a refutation.

## Examples & analogies

**The four-question test.** Faced with any numerical claim on the subject, ask: *which geographic perimeter?* (world or France) — *which denominator?* (accounting net income or adjusted net income) — *tax paid or tax charge in the accounts?* — *who really bears the burden?* (the company or the customer/employee). A source that does not let you answer all four is a slogan.

**The transfer pricing illustration.** The Shell case in Australia (2017-2024): gas bought for \$$83$bn from the Australian subsidiary, resold for \$$105$bn by the Singaporean subsidiary. The $\approx 26.5\ \%$ gap is not illegal — it is simply *located*. This is exactly the mechanism suspected of having an equivalent at Total via Geneva, without direct proof accessible from public documents.

**The total-contribution trap.** A supermarket that remits €$10$m of VAT has not "contributed" €$10$m: it took the money from customers and passed it on. Adding fuel excise duty and VAT to its own taxes is the same rhetorical figure.

## Where to look (primary sources)

- **Universal registration document (URD)**, filed each year with the French market regulator AMF (the 2025 one on 27 March 2026) — the tax note, effective rate, geographic breakdown. Paired with the *Form 20-F* for the SEC.
- **Report on payments to governments** (art. L225-102-3 of the French commercial code, EU accounting directive) — country-by-country payments, published since 2015. The most informative and least read document.
- **The group's tax transparency report**, voluntary since 2022 — this is where the "rest of the world" line sits.
- **Country-by-country reporting (CbCR)**, public in the EU from financial year 2026.
- On the public side: the French Court of Auditors, the Council of Compulsory Levies, finance committee reports (the Pouyanné hearing of 17 June 2026 is on video on the National Assembly's site).
- On the adversarial side: EU Tax Observatory / Gabriel Zucman and the Institut des politiques publiques (analytical), Observatoire des multinationales, Oxfam France, Attac (campaigning). Read them with the same demands about perimeter and denominator as the group's own documents.

## Open questions

- Can the "rest of the world" line be broken down from the Swiss and Singaporean statutory accounts, or is it structurally opaque?
- Is the Zucman proposal — taxing a fraction of *worldwide* windfall profits in proportion to turnover realised in France — compatible with bilateral tax treaties, or does it require denouncing them?
- Why is the France/Germany gap ($51\ \%$ of sales / $4\ \%$ of taxes against $19\ \%$ / $43\ \%$) so extreme? Activity mix, or location?
- Has Pillar 2 at $15\ \%$ raised the floor or enshrined a ceiling for trading activities?
- Is French refining loss-making for genuine industrial reasons (European overcapacity, cracking margins), or does the intra-group price structure contribute to it?
- Adjacent topics: unitary taxation with formulary apportionment, the tax regime for commodity trading in Switzerland, CCCTB/BEFIT at European level.
