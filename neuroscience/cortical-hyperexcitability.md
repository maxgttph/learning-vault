# Cortical Hyperexcitability

## TL;DR

> Hyperexcitability is the migraine brain's baseline trait: its neurons fire too easily (a lowered threshold) and its cortex fails to "tune out" repeated stimuli. It is a property of migraineurs **with and without aura** — not a one-off event but the fragile *terrain* on which attacks are triggered.

## Key concepts

- **Excitation/inhibition balance** — too much glutamate (accelerator), too little GABA (brake).
- **Channelopathy** — ion channels too permeable to $\text{Na}^+/\text{Ca}^{2+}$, or faulty $\text{K}^+$ channels.
- **Energy fragility** — underpowered $\text{Na}^+/\text{K}^+$ pump fails to clear extracellular $\text{K}^+$.
- **Habituation deficit** — the cortex amplifies rather than dampens repeated stimuli.
- **Fluctuating** — excitability rises in the days before an attack, then resets.

## Deep dive

### What "hyperexcitable" means

Building on [[neuron-function]]: a neuron fires when summed input crosses its **threshold**. Hyperexcitability means that threshold is **abnormally low** — less stimulation is needed to set off the discharge. Crucially, this is a *general* trait of the migraine brain, present in migraine **with and without aura**, not limited to those who get auras. It is the "dry forest"; cortical spreading depression (the aura event) is merely the "visible fire" that sometimes spreads across it.

### The mechanisms that go wrong

**1 — Glutamate/GABA imbalance.** Too much **glutamate** released and/or too little **GABA** (or weaker GABA receptors). The running sum of inputs is permanently tilted toward excitation, holding the membrane closer to threshold so neurons fire "for nothing".

**2 — Ion channels.** Often genetic. $\text{Na}^+$ or $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ channels that open too easily (too much depolarising charge in), or $\text{K}^+$ channels that repolarise poorly. More $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ entry also means **more glutamate released**, feeding mechanism 1. The demonstration case is familial hemiplegic migraine (genes *CACNA1A* = Ca²⁺ channel, *SCN1A* = Na⁺ channel, *ATP1A2* = Na/K pump).

**3 — Pumps and energy.** Weak mitochondria, magnesium deficiency, hypoglycaemia or poor sleep starve the $\text{Na}^+/\text{K}^+$ pump of ATP. Extracellular $\text{K}^+$ and glutamate are not cleared → accumulating $\text{K}^+$ depolarises neighbouring neurons → the substrate for a chain reaction. (This is why magnesium, riboflavin/B2, CoQ10 and regular meals are preventive.)

**4 — Habituation deficit.** Normally the cortex *reduces* its response to a **repeated** stimulus (protection against overload). The migraine cortex fails to, or amplifies instead. Everyday stimulation (screens, fluorescent light, noise) therefore **accumulates** rather than being filtered, driving excitation up until the cortex saturates and crosses threshold.

### Origins of the hyperexcitable terrain

- **Genetic** (dominant): polygenic in common migraine (many small-effect variants); monogenic channelopathies in rare familial hemiplegic migraine.
- **Neurotransmitter tilt**: excess glutamate / deficient GABA.
- **Fragile energy metabolism**: overstretched ion pumps.
- **Magnesium deficiency**: magnesium normally blocks the NMDA glutamate receptor; less of it = more excitation.
- **Hormones**: oestrogens modulate excitability; their drop (pre-menstrually) lowers the threshold.
- **Chronification / epigenetics**: repeated attacks sensitise the system — the brain "learns" to be sensitive.

## Examples & analogies

- **Dry forest vs fire**: every migraine forest is dry (excitable terrain); only some see a visible fire spread (CSD/aura).
- **Over-sensitive thermostat**: trips the heating at the slightest fluctuation — banal triggers (light, stress, missed meal) set it off.
- **A filter that stopped filtering** (habituation deficit): light, noise and screens pile up instead of being tuned out.

## Open questions

- Whether "silent" CSD occurs in clinically aura-free migraine.
- How exactly hormonal shifts retune excitability at the channel level.
