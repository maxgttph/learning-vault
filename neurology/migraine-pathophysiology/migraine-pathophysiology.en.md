# Migraine Pathophysiology

## TL;DR

> Migraine is a **neurovascular** disorder of a hyperexcitable brain — not primarily a vascular one. Several trigger pathways (cortical spreading depression, hypothalamus, brainstem, direct trigeminal activation) converge on **activation of the trigeminovascular system**, which releases **CGRP**, inflaming the meninges and sensitising their nerves. The pain comes from that inflammation + sensitisation, not from blood-vessel dilation itself.

## Key concepts

- **Trigeminovascular system** — trigeminal nerve endings wrapped around the meningeal vessels; the anatomical circuit of migraine pain.
- **Cortical spreading depression (CSD)** — a slow wave of cortical depolarisation (~$3$ mm/min); the substrate of **aura**.
- **CGRP** — *Calcitonin Gene-Related Peptide*; a potent vasodilator neuropeptide that propagates and amplifies the pain signal.
- **Nitric oxide (NO)** — a signalling molecule that *initiates* attacks (not merely a vasodilatory by-product).
- **Central sensitisation** — brainstem/thalamic relays become hypersensitive → allodynia.
- **Hypothalamus / brainstem** — non-CSD trigger pathways that activate the trigeminal system directly.

## Deep dive

### The old (wrong) vascular theory

Migraine was long blamed on vessels constricting (→ aura) then dilating (→ pulsatile pain). This is **outdated**. Dilation occurs but is a **consequence**, not the cause.

### The modern view: a neurovascular disorder

Migraine starts in a brain that is intrinsically **hyperexcitable** (see [[cortical-hyperexcitability.en]]). The cascade:

```
Hyperexcitable brain
        │
        ▼
(aura only) Cortical spreading depression
        │
        ▼
Activation of the trigeminal nerve (trigeminovascular system)
        │
        ▼
CGRP release → meningeal vasodilation + neurogenic inflammation
        │
        ▼
Sensitised nerve endings → pain signal
        │
        ▼
Brainstem → thalamus → cortex = PAIN (+ nausea, photophobia, allodynia)
```

**The brain itself feels no pain** (no nociceptors in brain tissue). The pain is in the **meninges and their vessels**, innervated by the trigeminal nerve.

### The trigeminovascular system

The convergence point of every trigger. Trigeminal endings coil around the meningeal vessels and work **bidirectionally**: they (1) carry pain *in* toward the brainstem nucleus, thalamus and cortex, and (2) release neuropeptides (chiefly CGRP) *out* on the spot → vasodilation + inflammation → more irritation → a self-sustaining loop.

### Aura is one entry door, not the only one

CSD produces aura and irritates the trigeminal endings — but it is only **one** way to switch on the pain. Roughly $1/3$ of migraineurs get aura. The **non-CSD pathways**:

- **Hypothalamic** (most important): the hypothalamus (sleep, hunger, hormones, day/night rhythm) activates the trigeminal nucleus directly via descending connections — no cortex, no wave, no aura. Explains the **prodrome** (yawning, food cravings, fatigue) appearing $24$–$48$ h before pain.
- **Brainstem**: dysregulated *locus coeruleus* (noradrenaline) and raphe nuclei (serotonin) lift the "brake" on the trigeminal nucleus.
- **Direct trigeminal activation/sensitisation**: internal chemistry (NO, CGRP, falling oestrogen, inflammation) or neighbouring pain input (jaw, neck) makes already-primed endings discharge.

Hyperexcitability does not necessarily *create* a CSD; it **lowers the threshold of all these doors at once**. All lead to the same room — trigeminal activation → CGRP → inflammation → pain.

### Why the pain is pulsatile

Dilated, inflamed meningeal vessels carry **sensitised** nerve endings. Each heartbeat's pressure wave **stretches** the vessel walls → the hypersensitive nerves are mechanically stimulated in rhythm → throbbing pain. (Central sensitisation also contributes, since the throb does not always match the pulse exactly.)

### Why the pain is usually one-sided

The whole chain is lateralised: a CSD spreads over **one** hemisphere; the trigeminal system is **paired** (a left and a right nerve, each serving its half of the meninges); the brainstem and thalamic relays are lateralised too. Hence typically **hemicranial** pain — though it can switch sides between attacks, and ~$1/3$ of attacks are bilateral. Strictly *side-locked* pain over years is a red flag warranting imaging.

### Pain source: inflammation + sensitisation, NOT vasodilation

A historically important correction. The driver is **neurogenic inflammation + nerve sensitisation**, not the dilation. Attacks can occur without significant dilation; effective drugs need not act by vasoconstriction. Dilation accompanies the picture (and feeds the throb) but is not the motor.

### NO and CGRP are initiators, not by-products

- **NO** is not just a vasodilator: it activates and sensitises the trigeminovascular system and promotes CGRP release. Infusing **nitroglycerin** (an NO donor) reliably *triggers a real migraine* hours later → NO is an initiator.
- **CGRP** is one of the body's strongest vasodilators, propagates the pain signal, is elevated in blood during attacks, and *injecting it triggers an attack* — near-direct proof of a causal role. This is why anti-CGRP drugs work (see [[migraine-treatments.en]]).

### How specific triggers create pain

- **Hunger/fasting → hypoglycaemia**: the energy-hungry ion pumps slow → $\text{K}^+$/glutamate accumulate → threshold for CSD/excitability drops; also activates the hypothalamus.
- **Fatigue/poor sleep**: sleep does cortical "clean-up" and energy recharge; deprivation leaves the cortex more excitable and stresses the hypothalamus. Both too little *and* too much sleep trigger.
- **Menstruation**: the pre-menstrual **drop in oestrogen** (the drop, not the low level) lowers the threshold and increases CGRP sensitivity → catamenial migraine.
- **Jaw/neck pain**: the jaw is innervated by the trigeminal nerve; chronic input bombards the (already sensitised) trigeminal nucleus, which shares the circuit with the meninges → spills over into the migraine pathway.

## Examples & analogies

- **A house with many doors to one room**: CSD is the visible glass door (you see who enters = aura); hypothalamus/brainstem/direct trigeminal are service doors (no one seen entering, but the room — the pain — fills anyway). Hyperexcitability = all the locks set too loose.
- **Nitroglycerin test**: giving NO on purpose reproduces a real attack — the cleanest demonstration that NO is upstream, not a side effect.

## Open questions

- Whether clinically aura-free migraine involves "silent" CSD in a mute cortical zone.
- The precise hypothalamic switch that starts attacks 24–48 h ahead.
- Long-term consequences of blocking protective CGRP/NO signalling.
