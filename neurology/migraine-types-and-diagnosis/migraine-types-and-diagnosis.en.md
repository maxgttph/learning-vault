# Migraine Types & Diagnosis

## TL;DR

> Migraine comes in many forms (with/without aura, episodic/chronic, hormonal, and several special variants). Diagnosis is **clinical** — built from the interview and a headache diary, not from a machine: there is no routine test that "sees" CSD or names a trigger. Mechanistic knowledge guides *what to look for*; the diary is the instrument that *detects*.

## Key concepts

- **Aura == CSD** — aura is the conscious manifestation of cortical spreading depression.
- **Episodic vs chronic** — chronic = ≥$15$ headache days/month (≥$8$ migrainous) for >$3$ months.
- **Catamenial migraine** — driven by the pre-menstrual oestrogen drop.
- **Red flags** — strictly side-locked pain, thunderclap onset, abnormal exam → image to exclude secondary causes.
- **Trigger identification** — observe triggers (diary), not mechanisms (unmeasurable individually).
- **Genetics** — polygenic in common migraine; monogenic only in rare hemiplegic forms.

## Deep dive

### Taxonomy

**By aura**
- **Without aura** (~$2/3$): typical attack (unilateral, pulsatile, worsened by effort, nausea, photo-/phonophobia), no preceding neurological symptoms.
- **With aura** (~$1/3$): transient neurological symptoms ($5$–$60$ min) before/at onset — **visual** (commonest), **sensory** (spreading tingling), or **aphasic**. Caused by CSD.

**By frequency**
- **Episodic**: <$15$ headache days/month.
- **Chronic**: ≥$15$/month (≥$8$ migrainous), >$3$ months. Often from "chronification" (central sensitisation), and beware **medication-overuse headache**.

**Hormonal**
- **Pure menstrual**: attacks *only* in the J−2 to J+3 window around menses onset.
- **Menstrually-related**: in that window *and* at other times (the commoner of the two). Mechanism: oestrogen drop.

**Special variants (atypical auras)**
- **Migraine with brainstem aura** (old "basilar"): vertigo, imbalance, double vision, slurred speech, reduced consciousness; usually benign.
- **Hemiplegic** (familial or sporadic): aura includes true **motor weakness** one side (stroke-mimic), lasting hours; genetic (*CACNA1A, ATP1A2, SCN1A*) — the model proving the channel role; diagnosis of exclusion.
- **Retinal/ocular**: visual disturbance (even transient blindness) in **one eye** only (≠ classic visual aura affecting both eyes' field).
- **Vestibular**: vertigo dominates, sometimes with little headache.
- **Abdominal** (mostly children): recurrent abdominal pain/nausea without headache; often a precursor of adult migraine.

**Acute complications**
- **Status migrainosus**: severe attack lasting >$72$ h despite treatment.
- **Persistent aura / migrainous infarction**: rare aura that fails to resolve, with true ischaemic lesion.

### Aura, CSD, and migraine without aura

**Aura == CSD** is accepted: aura is what living through a CSD feels like (visual cortex → shimmer; sensory cortex → tingling). But **migraine without aura ≠ definitely no CSD** — debated. Either there is genuinely no CSD (attack started via the hypothalamic/brainstem/direct-trigeminal routes, see [[migraine-pathophysiology.en]]), or a **"silent" CSD** occurs in a clinically mute cortical zone. Current stance: CSD is **necessary for aura but probably not for all migraine**. So aura ⟹ CSD, but migraine ⇏ necessarily CSD.

### Diagnosis is clinical, not instrumental

- **The interview is the master test.** Aura's presence and type localise the CSD (visual = occipital, sensory = parietal); its **slow spread** over minutes distinguishes it from a stroke (sudden, maximal at once).
- **CSD is essentially invisible in routine practice.** Standard **EEG** cannot reliably show it (the wave is too slow / near-DC and gets filtered out) — EEG is only for excluding epilepsy. **MEG** has captured CSD signatures and **fMRI** has *filmed* the spreading blood-flow wave during aura — but both are research tools, impractical in routine.
- **Imaging (MRI)** is to **exclude secondary causes**, not to see CSD. Red flags: strictly side-locked fixed pain, progressive worsening, abnormal neuro exam, thunderclap onset, unusual late age of onset.

### Finding a patient's triggers — the right method

Mechanisms (CSD, hypothalamus, channels, glutamate levels) are **not individually measurable** in a given patient, so you cannot "test each pathway". You work on what is observable instead:

1. **The observable layer is triggers, not mechanisms.** Keep a **headache diary** for 2–3 months (apps exist): per attack, log onset, intensity, duration, and the prior 24 h (sleep, meals/fasting, stress, hormonal cycle, alcohol, caffeine, jaw/neck, weather).
2. **Mechanistic knowledge tells you *what* to log and how to interpret** — energy (meals), sleep (excitability), hormones (cycle), jaw/neck (direct trigeminal input). It guides the diary; it does not replace it.
3. **Mind the traps**: correlation ≠ causation (chocolate/sugar craving is often a **prodrome**, not a trigger); attacks are usually **multifactorial** (the *sum* crosses threshold).
4. **Test by modification, not measurement**: fix one suspect (e.g. irregular sleep) for a few weeks and watch whether frequency drops — a clinical controlled experiment.

### Genetics is not an individual diagnostic tool

The migraine-channel genes (*CACNA1A, ATP1A2, SCN1A*) concern **hemiplegic** migraine (requires **motor** aura) — not common migraine. Even there a test is neither "clear" nor "certain": absence of mutation excludes nothing, variants of unknown significance abound, penetrance is incomplete. Common migraine is **polygenic** (a diffuse *risk*, not a *cause*). Genetic testing is indicated only for hemiplegic forms (genetic counselling / difficult differential), not routine migraine.

### Worked clinical reasoning (illustrative)

A woman with a strong attack on Monday and menses starting Thursday (attack at J−3) fits the **pre-menstrual oestrogen-drop** window — *if recurrent* over 2–3 cycles (one coincidence proves nothing; the diary decides). A history of stopping the combined pill at 17 for **visual disturbance + vertigo** is telling on two counts: those are likely **aura** symptoms (visual + vertigo suggests **brainstem aura**), revealed/worsened by the pill's oestrogen; and crucially, **migraine with aura + combined (oestrogen) contraception raises ischaemic-stroke risk** — combined pills are contraindicated, favouring oestrogen-free options. The picture: hormone-sensitive migraine with aura, with a contraceptive-safety implication — to confirm with a diary and by clarifying whether auras occur.

## Examples & analogies

- **Detective work, not a scan**: origin is reconstructed from phenotype + timeline + diary, not read off a machine.
- **Side-locked = look underneath**: a true migraine may switch sides; pain obstinately fixed warrants imaging.

## Open questions

- Whether silent CSD underlies clinically aura-free attacks.
- Biomarkers that could one day make trigger-pathway attribution objective.
